## About the experiment

In this experiment, two matches are arranged so that the coin can be released only after the setup is heated.

> [!CAUTION]
> This experiment uses an open flame, matches, and scissors. Perform it only under adult supervision and away from flammable materials. Do not touch burned matches immediately.

## Experiment

1. Carefully make a small hole in the matchbox.
2. If the match does not fit, slightly widen the hole with an adult's help.
3. Secure the first match in the box.
4. Place the coin near the first match.
5. Carefully position the second match as required by the experiment.
6. With an adult's help, light the matches.
7. Keep your hands away and observe the motion.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not hold your face directly above the experiment, and do not try to pick up the coin until the flame is fully extinguished and the parts have cooled.

## How does it work?

When the matches burn, their wood and outer coating become very hot. During heating, part of the wood dries, chars, and loses its original mechanical strength.

Because the match does not heat equally on all sides, different parts change by different amounts. This uneven heating and charring can cause the match to **bend**.

In this experiment, the bending of the second match changes the position in which it holds the coin. Once it bends enough, the coin is no longer held in the same way and can be released.

> [!NOTE]
> The main phenomenon here is not the heating of the coin, but the change in the structure of the match wood during heating and combustion.
