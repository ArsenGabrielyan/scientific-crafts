## About the experiment

This simple experiment lets you observe how water moves through the fibers of a paper towel and carries marker dyes with it, creating a colorful rainbow-like image.

## Experiment

![Colors on the paper strip](/images/make-a-rainbow/tissue-example.jpg)

1. Cut a long strip from a paper towel with scissors.
2. A little above the bottom of the strip, make colored dots with markers in this order:
   - Red
   - Orange
   - Yellow
   - Green
   - Light blue
   - Blue
   - Purple
3. Pour a small amount of water into the glass.
4. Place the lower edge of the paper strip in the water so that the colored dots remain above the water level.
5. Wait and observe how the water gradually rises through the paper.
6. Watch how the colors spread and move with the water.

> [!TIP]
> Use water-based markers. The experiment may not work with permanent markers or markers whose ink does not dissolve in water.

## How does it work?

A paper towel is made of many fibers with very small spaces between them. Water can move through these spaces because of **capillary action**.

When the rising water reaches the marker dots, water-soluble dyes begin to dissolve and move with the water through the paper.

This phenomenon is also related to **paper chromatography**. Different dyes can move through the paper at different rates depending on their solubility in water and their interaction with the paper fibers.

> [!NOTE]
> No chemical reaction that produces a new substance takes place here. The experiment mainly demonstrates water movement, solubility, and the separation and spreading of dyes.

## What can you test?

Repeat the experiment with different types of markers or change the order of the colors. Compare which colors spread faster and whether some colors separate into several different shades.
