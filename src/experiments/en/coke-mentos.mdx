## About the experiment

In this well-known experiment, dropping Mentos candies into a carbonated drink causes a large amount of foam to erupt rapidly from the bottle.

> [!NOTE]
> The original video for this experiment was published in 2020 under the title “Volcanic Reaction.” The more accurate title “Coca-Cola and Mentos Eruption” is used here.

> [!CAUTION]
> Perform this experiment only outdoors and under adult supervision. The eruption can happen very quickly and reach a considerable height. Do not hold your face over the bottle.

## Experiment

1. Place the Coca-Cola bottle upright on a flat, stable outdoor surface.
2. Open the bottle while disturbing it as little as possible.
3. Prepare several Mentos in advance so you can drop them into the bottle quickly.
4. Drop the Mentos into the bottle all at once.
5. Move away from the bottle immediately.
6. Observe the eruption.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not close the bottle after adding the Mentos. Rapid gas release inside a sealed container can create dangerous pressure.

## How does it work?

Coca-Cola is a carbonated drink containing a large amount of **carbon dioxide (CO₂)** dissolved under pressure. When the bottle is opened, the pressure decreases and the gas can gradually leave the liquid.

Although the surface of Mentos looks smooth, it contains many tiny irregularities. These become **nucleation sites**, where bubbles can form.

When several Mentos enter the drink at once, many CO₂ bubbles form very rapidly on their surfaces. The bubbles rise and expand, carrying liquid upward with them. As a result, liquid and gas are forced out through the narrow bottle opening at high speed, creating the eruption.

> [!NOTE]
> The main effect is not a chemical reaction between Coca-Cola and Mentos that creates a new substance. The eruption is primarily caused by the extremely rapid release of CO₂ that was already dissolved in the drink.

## What can you test?

You can compare different carbonated drinks or change the number of Mentos and observe how the strength and height of the eruption change.

> [!TIP]
> Change only one condition at a time when comparing results. For example, if you compare different drinks, try to use bottles of the same size and the same number of Mentos.
