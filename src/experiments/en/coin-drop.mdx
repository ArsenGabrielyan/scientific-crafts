## About the experiment

This simple experiment demonstrates **Newton's first law**, also known as the law of inertia.

The coin is initially at rest. When the playing card is removed very quickly from underneath it, the coin tends to preserve its initial state and does not move along with the card.

## Experiment

1. Place the playing card over the opening of the glass.
2. Place the coin in the center of the card.
3. Strike the edge of the card sharply so it moves away quickly in a horizontal direction.
4. Observe how the coin falls into the glass.

> [!TIP]
> You can repeat the experiment with several coins stacked on top of one another. Try removing the card with one sharp motion and observe the result.

## How does it work?

According to Newton's first law, an object remains at rest or continues moving uniformly in a straight line unless an external force causes that state to change.

When the card is struck sharply, the force acts mainly on the **card**. Because the card moves away very quickly, friction between the card and the coin acts on the coin for only a very short time.

Because of its **inertia**, the coin initially stays almost in the same place.

Once the card is gone, the coin no longer has support underneath it, and **Earth's gravitational force** pulls it downward into the glass.

### Newton's first law

$$
\sum \vec{F} = 0
$$

where $\sum \vec{F}$ is the vector sum of the forces acting on the object.

If the net force on an object is zero, its velocity does not change: it either remains at rest or continues moving at constant velocity in a straight line.

> [!NOTE]
> Newton's first law is not a law of gravity. Here, inertia explains why the coin does not move with the card, while gravity explains why it then falls into the glass.

## What can you test?

Try coins with different masses, a stack of several coins, or different types of cards. Also compare slow and sharp movements: when the card is pulled slowly, friction acts on the coin for much longer, changing the result.
